# Research digests (7 areas, each web-researched then fact-checked; 'Corrections' are the fact-checker's changes to assumptions)


## Gemma 4 model family and runtimes (as of 2026-10-18)

### Digest
What exists. Gemma 4 (Apache 2.0, so no license worries for a hackathon demo or a product) is five models: E2B, E4B, 12B Unified, 26B-A4B MoE, 31B, released March 31 2026 with the 12B added June 3 and EmbeddingGemma 2 (740M, text+image+video+audio embeddings) on October 6. Only E2B, E4B and 12B hear audio; nothing in the family speaks, so a TTS (Kokoro or Kyutai Pocket TTS are the proven Jetson choices) is a separate component. Audio is clip-based, not streaming: 16 kHz, 40 ms per token, hard 30 s cap, trained for transcription and transcribe-then-translate. Quality is "good small ASR" rather than Whisper-large: FLEURS WER 0.090 (E2B) / 0.075 (E4B), best in Spanish/Italian/Portuguese (0.03-0.06), weakest in Arabic and Chinese (CER 0.19 for E2B), X-to-English CoVoST BLEU 35/38. E4B is roughly 15-25% better at speech than E2B across the board, which matters for a multilingual concierge. Thinking is a <|think|> system token; tool calls are a trained six-token format that llama.cpp (--jinja), vLLM (--tool-call-parser gemma4) and LiteRT-LM all parse natively. Video is frames at 1 fps (70 tokens/frame budget).

What runs where. The best news for us is verified locally: the exact mmproj-BF16.gguf the harness already loads (unsloth E2B QAT) and Google's official E4B QAT mmproj both carry the gemma4a audio encoder (751 audio tensors, 12 conformer blocks), and llama.cpp has supported Gemma 4 audio since April 12 (PR #21421, regression fixed June 4). So run_llama.sh as it stands can accept a WAV through the OpenAI-compatible input_audio part today; the PR recommends keeping the projector at BF16 and the harness should disable thinking (E2B otherwise burns its budget and returns empty content). On the Orin the picture is mixed: NVIDIA's Jetson AI Lab says llama.cpp has "a known audio issue with E2B on Orin" and points to its vLLM container, while a community Orin Nano Super project runs native speech into E2B on llama.cpp CUDA (arch 87) with 0.4-0.6 s time-to-first-token in a 5.7 GiB GPU budget. Treat that as the first thing to test on PC2. TensorRT Edge-LLM lists E2B/E4B/12B with audio and measures ~32 tok/s (E2B) and ~24 tok/s (E4B W4A16) decode on an Orin NX 16 GB, but E4B's FP16 embedding tables leave only ~3 GB free, so on the NX prefer E2B, or E4B only if nothing else needs memory. Ollama's audio path is still crashing (issue open since April) and it does not run Gemma 4 on Orin Nano at all; skip it. MLX and Transformers both do audio and are fine for Mac-side development.

LiteRT-LM (v0.18.0, Oct 6) is production-quality on phones, laptops and Raspberry Pi, has a clean Python API (Engine with backend/audio_backend/vision_backend, Content.AudioFile, Python functions as tools, ThinkingConfig), a CLI with --attachment=audio.wav and an automatic tool loop, and an OpenAI-compatible serve mode. Official E2B/E4B/12B bundles exist with on-demand vision+audio, plus NPU builds for Qualcomm, Tensor G5/G6 and Intel; there is no NVIDIA build. On Jetson it is officially benchmarked (Orin Nano GPU: 24 tok/s decode, 0.9 s TTFT) but the linux_arm64 GPU path is a WebGPU accelerator that users reported crashing on Orin from June through August; a September report says it works on an Orin NX 16 GB after manually fetching the prebuilt/linux_arm64 libraries. CPU fallback works but is ~12 tok/s class.

Robotics. There is no open Gemma robotics model: Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 (July 30) is Gemma-based but trusted-tester only, bi-arm, and explicitly weak on high-DoF bodies. The open route for a G1 is LeRobot's official Unitree G1 support (teleop with an exoskeleton, record, pi0.5 fine-tune, RTC rollout), which is a multi-day data effort, not a six-day one.

Recommendation. Make Gemma 4 the whole cognitive loop, which is exactly what the Gemma team is showcasing right now (their late-August post: E2B on an 8 GB Orin Nano with native image/audio input, function calling, voice interaction and camera control; NVIDIA's reference stack is Parakeet STT -> E2B -> look_and_answer tool -> Kokoro). We can beat that reference by removing the STT: feed the microphone clip straight into Gemma (VAD-chunked under 30 s, audio placed after the text, image before it), let one model transcribe, understand, pick a language, and emit the robot tool call, and keep the measured sub-second perception path for navigation. Use E2B on PC2 for latency and memory headroom, E4B only if speech accuracy in non-English demos needs it. For the LiteRT prize, do not bet the robot on LiteRT-LM-on-Orin; instead put LiteRT-LM where it is solid and judge-visible: EmbeddingGemma 2 via litert-lm serve as the robot's multimodal memory ("where did you see my keys?", search over what the camera and microphone captured), and/or Gemma 4 E2B on a Pixel/Raspberry Pi as the concierge front-end that talks to the robot over the LAN. Test LiteRT-LM GPU on PC2 on day one anyway; if it runs, that becomes the headline. Judges from the Gemma team will respond to: native audio in, native tool calling, EmbeddingGemma 2 (two weeks old), everything offline, and measured latencies on the slide.

### Corrections from the fact-checker
Five of six claims hold; change these assumptions. (1) Audio scope is wider than "ASR/AST only": E2B answers spoken questions and extracts JSON from spoken commands directly (verified live on this Mac: "What is the capital of France?" -> "Paris" with no text prompt; soda request -> {"object":"cold can of soda","location":"fridge"}, 232-511 ms wall). Design the voice loop as speech -> E2B -> action, not speech -> ASR -> text -> E2B. But multilingual S2TT on E2B was poor in a one-shot Spanish trial, so evaluate each demo language before promising translation on stage. (2) CoVoST: the model card (33.47/35.54/38.5) and tech report (35.4/38.2) disagree; cite one source. (3) llama.cpp: the BF16-mmproj rule is obsolete (root cause fixed Apr 13 by #21824; Q8_0 mmproj works and is 557 MB), #21625 was a prerequisite not a follow-up, Metal now has a ROLL kernel, and the current harness server already accepts audio (same model, no downloads), logging "audio input is in experimental stage". Audio is 25 tokens/s (official), must be mono 16 kHz, >30 s is chunked by llama.cpp but truncated by Transformers; identical clips are KV-cached, so rotate clips when timing. (4) NVIDIA's Orin note is undated, unlinked and E2B-only; the Aug 31 community repo runs native E2B audio on llama.cpp 9f0d017 (Aug 17, after all fixes) on an Orin Nano Super with 0.37-0.56 s TTFT, and its only llama.cpp patch is a TTS server. NVIDIA's own Docker llama.cpp image is text-only (no mmproj), a plausible but unconfirmed origin of the "issue". Plan a native CUDA (arch 87) build from a recent commit on PC2 first; vLLM gemma4-jetson-orin container is the fallback, and Ollama is reported not to work with Gemma 4 on Orin Nano. (5) Docs moved to gemma.ai; license is plain Apache 2.0 (GGUF metadata agrees). Note: the shared web-search budget was exhausted, so verification used direct fetches of official docs, GitHub/HF APIs, GGUF header parsing and a live server test.

- The runtime behind Google's late-August Jetson Orin Nano tweet (native audio + function calling + camera control) is unknown; the only public reference stack (NVIDIA HF blog) uses llama.cpp with a separate Parakeet STT. If the Gemma team's own demo code surfaces, mirror its prompt/tool structure.
- TensorRT Edge-LLM on a 16 GB NX: E4B leaves ~3.1 GB headroom and E2B ~5.9 GB with FP16 embedding sidecars (issue #188 still open); only worth pursuing if llama.cpp decode on PC2 is too slow for the tool-call budget.

## LiteRT and LiteRT-LM platform support (as of 2026-10-18)

### Digest
What exists. LiteRT is the renamed TensorFlow Lite runtime (2.3.0, Oct 7 2026; Python package ai-edge-litert) and LiteRT-LM (0.18.0, Oct 6 2026) is the LLM layer on top of it. It is now Google's only recommended on-device LLM path: the MediaPipe LLM Inference API is "maintenance-only" and the AI Edge Function Calling SDK is "Deprecated... migrate to LiteRT-LM". Install is `pip install litert-lm litert-lm-api` (Python >=3.10). The bindings ship manylinux_2_27 aarch64 wheels, so they install on the G1's Orin NX (JetPack 6, Ubuntu 22.04) with no build; do not build from source, the prebuilt constraint-provider library has an ABI mismatch that aborts tool calls (#3821). No jetson-containers package exists; pip is the route. APIs: Python, Kotlin and C++ stable; Swift and JS early preview. Models: litert-community/gemma-4-E2B-it-litert-lm (2.58 GB) and E4B (3.65 GB), mixed 2/4/8-bit, vision and audio encoders inside the file and loaded on demand, 32k context, MTP drafter included; 12B is laptop-only; 26B/31B unsupported. Audio input is 16 kHz mono, 30 s max, 25 tokens/s, multilingual ASR and translation. It is input only: Gemma has no speech output, so a talking robot needs a separate on-device TTS.

Where it runs. CPU (XNNPACK) everywhere. GPU: OpenCL on Android, Metal on Apple, WebGPU (Dawn) over Vulkan on Linux x86_64/arm64 and Windows. NPU: Qualcomm, MediaTek, Google Tensor G5/G6, Intel, Samsung. There is no CUDA or TensorRT path in LiteRT or LiteRT-LM, so on Jetson the GPU is reachable only through Vulkan/WebGPU.

Jetson numbers. Google's E2B model card lists Jetson Orin Nano: CPU 109 prefill / 12.2 decode tok/s (TTFT 9.4 s on a 1024-token prompt), GPU 1,142 / 24.2 tok/s (TTFT 0.9 s, 2.7 GB). Community reality on Orin NX 16 GB: the pip wheel's GPU backend worked in April (about 1.9 s per short text command, 3 GB RSS, 5 s engine init), failed in WebGPU graph compilation for the official E2B bundle in June through August (issue closed without a fix), and in September E4B on GPU worked again only after downloading prebuilt/linux_arm64 and setting LD_LIBRARY_PATH. Treat Jetson CPU as reliable (expect roughly 12-18 tok/s with 8 threads) and Jetson GPU as "probably, after a day of testing". The one Jetson head-to-head (Thor) had llama.cpp CUDA at about 80 tok/s versus LiteRT-LM about 21 tok/s on CPU. Vision is the bigger problem for our loop: LiteRT-LM's Gemma 4 vision encoder costs about 8 s per image on ARM CPUs at the default ~280-token budget (RK3588, QCS8275), 5.4 s on a Mali GPU, and nobody has published Jetson vision timings. The Python API does expose our 70-token trick: Engine(max_vision_tokens_per_image=70) plus create_conversation(visual_token_budget=70), options 70/140/280/560/1120.

Features that matter. Tool use: pass plain Python functions as tools=[fn]; the runtime builds the schema from the docstring and auto-executes calls, with an approve_tool_call hook. Tool-call syntax is constrained natively, and LLGuidance adds Regex/JSON-schema/Lark constraints: ResponseFormat.json(schema) with ConstrainedDecodingConfig(provider=LL_GUIDANCE). Thinking is a per-message toggle with a token budget (0 = off). MTP (enable_speculative_decoding) is claimed at up to 2.2x on GPU, 1.5x on CPU. `litert-lm serve` is an OpenAI-compatible server (port 9379; streaming chat completions and, since 0.18.0, /v1/embeddings). Gotchas: always pass audio_backend and vision_backend or Python segfaults; keep one persistent engine (5 s init); config lives in ~/.litert-lm/config.json (backend, cpu_thread_count, cache, max_num_tokens, thinking...).

Other hardware. Raspberry Pi 5: E2B at 133/7.6 tok/s CPU (Google's Aug 2026 blog: 99/9 tok/s, 1.4 GB), and Google's own showcase is a Reachy Mini robot on a Pi 5 running YOLO (GPU) + Moonshine ASR + Gemma 4 E2B through LiteRT-LM, the closest public precedent to our demo. Pixel 11 (Tensor G6): a Gemma 4 E2B NPU build exists (4-bit, 8192 ctx, 2.6 GB), the Tensor SDK Beta v2.1 (Oct 14 2026, sign-up required) includes a sample app running multimodal Gemma 4 on the Pixel TPU, but no Gemma 4 tok/s is published for any Pixel; EmbeddingGemma 2 on Pixel 11 Pro TPU embeds text in 8.3 ms.

Recommendation. Keep llama.cpp+CUDA as the sub-second perception loop on the Orin (our measured path; LiteRT-LM cannot match it) and give LiteRT-LM a separate, visible job judges can point at: (1) the speech and planning brain on the Orin, Gemma 4 E2B via litert-lm-api with 30-s audio clips (multilingual commands) emitting tool calls under constrained decoding, running on CPU with GPU as a bonus; (2) EmbeddingGemma 2 (485 MB, text+image+audio) via LiteRT-LM as the robot's on-device memory/search of what it has seen and heard; (3) optionally a Pi 5 or Pixel 11 companion running Gallery/LiteRT-LM as the user-facing concierge terminal (Pi 5 for the Raspberry Pi prize at ~8 tok/s; Pixel for Best in Show/TPU, unverified). Day-1 test on the Orin: pip install, run E2B on CPU with --cpu-thread-count=8 under MAXN, then --backend=gpu (fallback: prebuilt/linux_arm64 + LD_LIBRARY_PATH), measure text, a 3-s wav and an image at visual_token_budget 70 vs 280. GPU working means ~24 tok/s and sub-second TTFT for short prompts; CPU only means ~12-15 tok/s and multi-second vision, fine for speech and planning, not for the fast loop.

### Corrections from the fact-checker
Change these assumptions. (1) LiteRT-LM GPU on the Orin NX is officially unsupported, not merely flaky: the LiteRT-LM matrix (updated 2026-10-06) lists IoT as CPU-only, the only Linux arm64 accelerator is WebGPU via Dawn on Vulkan, and both Jetson issues (#2570, #2338) were closed in July 2026 without a fix. The one positive report (Sep 14, E4B, LD_LIBRARY_PATH pointing at a downloaded prebuilt/linux_arm64) names no version. A plausible cause the researcher missed: python/litert_lm/BUILD bundles only libLiteRt, libLiteRtWebGpuAccelerator, libLiteRtTopKWebGpuSampler, libGemmaModelConstraintProvider and liblitert-lm into the Linux wheels, not libwebgpu_dawn.so, which appeared in prebuilt/linux_arm64 around Jun 8, 2026, after the April success on 0.10.1. Day-one test on the robot: pip install litert-lm, ldd the accelerator, retry GPU with LD_LIBRARY_PATH at the prebuilt directory. Keep llama.cpp/CUDA as the sub-second perception engine; budget LiteRT-LM CPU near the Orin Nano figures (E2B 109/12.2 tok/s, 9.4 s TTFT at 1024 tokens) unless GPU works, and try enable_ynnpack (arm64 wheels build with YNNPACK since v0.16.0). (2) Larger Gemma 4 bundles exist: 12B (6.9 GB, text+vision+audio, v0.17+, ~7.5 GB GPU memory) and text-only 26B A4B/31B (15.8/19.2 GB, desktop v0.17.1+ and web, GPU only); none fits the Orin plan. (3) Audio is input-only on E2B/E4B/12B (16 kHz, 30 s, 25 tok/s); speech output needs separate TTS. (4) JetPack 7.2/7.2.1 (L4T 39.2, Ubuntu 24.04) now covers the Orin family, but an Orin Nano on R39.2 also failed on GPU in July, so do not upgrade six days out expecting a fix. (5) Pre-download every bundle and wheel; the event forbids cloud. ai.google.dev Gemma docs are marked deprecated in favor of gemma.ai. WebSearch was unavailable this turn, so every check used direct fetches of official pages, PyPI metadata and the repository.

## Unitree G1 hardware and software (as relevant to a voice + vision + manipulation demo)

### Digest

## Prior art and demo-primitive feasibility

### Digest
What made humanoid+LLM demos land, and what made service robots boring. Figure 01 (2024), Helix (2025), Digit's LLM demo and Google's Apollo demos all share three things: language that is visibly grounded in what the robot sees at that moment, a completed physical act (apple handover, grocery pass between robots, box moved), and credible autonomy. Tesla's We Robot and 1X NEO lost the room the instant teleoperation was suspected. Pepper, temi and the Henn-na Hotel staff were retired because they talked but neither understood people nor touched objects. For six judges rotating science-fair style, the winning shape is therefore: a judge speaks (in any language), the robot shows it heard and saw correctly within about a second, then does one unmistakable physical thing, with no laptop operator in the loop. Google's own event copy ("learns a chore from watching you once") echoes the Oct 14 Gemini 4 Argon cornhole demo and Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 (<200 examples, hours of adaptation, early access only); neither is reproducible in six days, but a SeeDo-style version is.

What runs where. Speech in: Gemma 4 E2B/E4B hear 16 kHz audio up to 30 s through a 305M conformer; FLEURS WER is 8.0%/6.5% English and 9.0%/7.5% across 12 languages including zh, ja, ko, es, hi, ar. llama.cpp has it (PR #21421 merged April 12, E2B fix #21824, llama-server `input_audio` working by June) and one CUDA user measured RTF ~0.04 with E4B Q4_K_M, but E2B Q8_0 leaked thinking tokens (RTF 0.3, HTTP 500s), a Japanese test via Ollama looped and hallucinated, and Jetson AI Lab flags "an audio issue with E2B under llama.cpp" and points to its vLLM container for audio on Orin. So: headline the single-model story (Gemma hears, sees, decides) but ship a tested fallback. On the G1's own Orin NX, faster-whisper base fp16 does a 5.5 s utterance in 0.37 s; sherpa-onnx lists Orin NX with GPU and bundles Whisper, Parakeet v3 (25 European languages, not streaming), Moonshine, VAD, and text-defined keyword spotting with no training. Speech out: Piper (39 languages, no Japanese/Korean, fast on CPU, binary works on aarch64) is the safe default; Kokoro has better English and Japanese but its Mandarin voices are graded D and it is slow on small CPUs. The G1 speaker is reachable via AudioClient.PlayStream over DDS; Unitree's own TtsMaker exists too.

Audio capture is the known time sink. Marvik spent a month on it: the G1's 4-mic array picks up Jetson, motor-driver and vibration noise and software fixes did not help; an external USB mic placed away from the electronics did. Their custom openWakeWord model hit 57% recall with 11 false wakes per hour, so they switched to Porcupine. In a loud hall, use a ReSpeaker XVF3800 (USB Audio Class 2.0, AEC, beamforming, direction-of-arrival to the host, 5 m) mounted on the chest, or clip a USB-class-compliant wireless lavalier on the judge, and keep push-to-talk (a button on the robot, or the judge's phone over our own AP) as the zero-risk path. Direction-of-arrival from the array is a free "turn to face whoever spoke" primitive.

Vision: our box_2d path already scores 100% at 378 ms at 70 image tokens on the Mac, and Google's doc shows the 70-token budget misses objects in clutter (2 of 6 versus 6 at 280), so use 140-280 tokens or a crop for table scenes. NanoOWL's famous 95 FPS is AGX-only with no NX number and community reports of seconds per frame when misconfigured; Grounding DINO 1.5 Edge is API-gated; YOLO-World worldv2 with set_classes exports to TensorRT but has no Jetson figure (closed-set YOLO26n is 4 ms on NX). Recommendation: keep Gemma 4 as the only perception model for the demo logic and add YOLO-World only if a 10 Hz tracker proves necessary.

Robot primitives available today without learning: Move(vx, vy, vyaw), WaveHand, ShakeHand, and 16 canned arm actions (hug, high five, clap, heart, hands up, reject...). Reaching needs the xr_teleoperate pinocchio IK plus a D435i depth target; no sourced autonomous G1 tabletop grasp exists, and RealSense on Orin NX under JetPack 6 needs a source build. Treat grasp as the risk item and design the demo so everything before it already impresses.

Feasibility-ranked primitives (stack in parentheses): 1) voice Q&A about the live scene with gesture replies (ext. mic, PTT or sherpa KWS, Gemma 4 audio+image via llama-server or faster-whisper fallback, Piper, AudioClient, ExecuteAction) - high. 2) multilingual understanding and reply (Gemma 4 ASR/AST; Piper for es/zh/fr/de/hi/ar, Kokoro for ja) - high, verify each language on the shipped quant. 3) gestures - trivial, but verify WaveHand on our firmware. 4) point at / identify objects (box_2d to bearing, turn plus say) - high. 5) walk-to-person (box_2d bearing and box height, Move until LiDAR/depth stop distance, person with the remote on L2+B) - medium-high. 6) learn-from-watching (hand-speed keyframes, Gemma 4 lists objects and steps, map to existing primitives, replay with pointing or a simple pick-place) - medium; keep to 2-3 steps. 7) grasp a known object from a table (depth, IK, fixed-height close on a soft can) - medium-low. 8) handover (extend arm, release when a hand is detected) - depends on 7.

Mitigations: wired or own 5 GHz AP, code freeze a day before, two charged batteries (vendor says ~2 h), one person holding the remote, even overhead lighting, and pre-recorded voice lines for the long-latency path.

### Corrections from the fact-checker
All six claims survive, but four planning assumptions must change. (1) Nobody has demonstrated Gemma 4 audio on an Orin NX: Marvik's conversational G1 ran Whisper, the LLM and TTS on an external 2x A40 host with only wake-word, mic and speaker on PC2; every llama.cpp audio report is x86 CUDA or Apple Metal; and NVIDIA's recipe flags an undated E2B audio problem under llama.cpp (cause unknown; its JetPack 6 images b8966/b9066 postdate the April fix). Validate on-Orin ASR first, with a USB mic (Marvik found the onboard array unusable), a BF16/F16 mmproj and thinking off. (2) Containers: `ghcr.io/nvidia-ai-iot/llama_cpp:latest-jetson-orin` now resolves to a JetPack-7 image (r38.2, CUDA 13), outside the JetPack 6 CUDA compatibility table; pin `b9066-r36.4.tegra-aarch64-cu129-22.04` (CUDA 12.9 toolkits are supported on the JetPack 6 driver). (3) TensorRT Edge-LLM needs JetPack 7.2; JetPack 7.2.1 does list the Orin family, so it is reachable, but only by reflashing Unitree's PC2 image six days out, which is not worth the risk. (4) The no-cloud rule: Unitree's `voice` service exposes TtsMaker and a `rt/audio_msg` ASR topic, but nothing documents where that ASR/TTS runs; prove it works with WAN unplugged or avoid it, using Gemma 4 ASR plus a local TTS (Piper, as unitree_converse does) through PlayStream (16 kHz mono int16). Smaller fixes: llama-server ignores `input_audio.format`; mics and speaker are standard on every G1, not EDU-only; no pointing output format exists anywhere, so plan on box_2d only; at the 70-token budget detection misses objects in clutter, so budget 140-280 tokens or crop for table scenes (the harness's 100% is one large fridge). Unverified: the NVIDIA forum NanoOWL latencies (HTTP 403), and web search was unavailable this run, so all checks used direct fetches.

## Hardware-partner award stacking (Raspberry Pi, Coral/Synaptics, Arduino/Qualcomm, Nvidia, Pixel purchase and lead times)

### Digest
What the partner awards actually are. The event page lists cash only for Best in Show (Android/Pixel), the two tracks and LiteRT; the partner prizes are hardware (5x Pi 5, 5x Coralboards, 2x Pixel 11, two Qualcomm packages) and none of them has published criteria. Nvidia and Synaptics are sponsors without their own award; Synaptics' award is in effect the Coralboard, since the new Coralboard is a Synaptics SL2619 board carrying Google Research's Coral NPU. What runs where, with numbers. Raspberry Pi 5 is Google's reference LiteRT IoT board. Gemma 4 E2B via LiteRT-LM on the Pi 5 CPU does 99-133 tok/s prefill and 7.6-9 tok/s decode in about 1.4-1.5 GB, with TTFT around 8 s for a 1024-token prompt, so prompts must stay short; Gemma 3 270M does 23-39 tok/s decode in 680 MB. The CLI takes .jpg/.wav attachments (`--attachment`, `--audio-backend=cpu`), has function calling (`--preset`), MTP, and an OpenAI-compatible `serve`. The GPU path exists but is slower than CPU for these models. The AI HAT+ 2 (Hailo-10H, $130) has no Gemma in its zoo, was measured slower than the Pi CPU for LLMs, and Google says LiteRT/Gemma on Hailo is only "coming soon"; buy it only if you want YOLO at 30 fps, and even then the AI Camera ($70) or the Pi CPU (YOLO26n at 101 ms) is simpler. The UNO Q ($79, in stock) runs Gemma 3 1B on four A53 cores at 5.5 tok/s through App Lab's llama.cpp brick with no NPU; it is a real-time I/O board (STM32 side) more than an AI board. The VENTUNO Q is the only Qualcomm part that runs Gemma 4 on an NPU (LiteRT-LM lists Dragonwing IQ8 at 3,747/31.7 tok/s and GenieX supports QCS8275), but it is a $299 pre-order with 3-4 week delivery, so it is unobtainable by Oct 24. On our own Jetson Orin NX, LiteRT-LM is confirmed working CPU-side (text and audio) and GPU-side via the Python wheel on an Orin NX 16 GB, with a known LD_LIBRARY_PATH workaround for newer builds; llama.cpp with CUDA remains faster (Thor: 80 vs 15-24 tok/s).
